Groove

Rhythm styles written as grids of chances: each bar is drawn again from the grid, so the groove stays itself without ever repeating exactly. Pick two styles and morph from one to the other, like a band changing groove.

Play a melody

Octave 0

Free: you play. Guide: a melody is written for the groove, silently; its notes fall on the stage and light the keys ahead of time, and you play them (Perfect, Good, Miss). Auto: the melody plays itself. C switches. Sing: hum or sing into the microphone, the instrument plays your note, moved to the nearest note of the scale (best with headphones, so the mic doesn't hear the music). The two letter rows play the groove's scale, so there is no wrong note. Ringed keys: the root. Dotted keys: the three notes of the chord playing now, good places to land. The top row is the middle row an octave up.

Loop & build

Record what you sing or play (it switches the microphone on; the letter keys work too): it starts on the next "one", lasts the chosen length, snaps to the beat, then loops in the instrument you were using. The first loop is your theme: with Build on, each bar gets the chord that fits it best, and the scale stays the one you sang in. Record again to stack layers (up to 4), each in the instrument selected at that moment.

Styles

Morph hands the instruments over one at a time, on the bar: hats, percussion, keys, bass, then the beat itself, announced by a drum fill while the tempo slides.

Each style brings its tempo, swing, chord mode and sounds (the status line shows the key and mode). The chords' mode follows the style playing the keys.

Instruments

Drums are synthesised (no samples). The styles live in groove/styles.js, as readable grids: edit one, refresh, listen.
